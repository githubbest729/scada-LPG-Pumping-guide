Bulk LPG transfer skid for Dubai. Use this as a design checklist, then confirm every value against the project specification and the authority having jurisdiction.

Process flow

  1. Bulk storage. Mounded or above-ground bullet tanks. Liquid outlet through an internal excess-flow valve, then a fail-closed suction ESDV.
  2. Pump. Positive displacement (sliding vane, such as Corken) or regenerative turbine (such as Blackmer). Mount below the tank liquid level to keep suction head positive.
  3. Bypass DPV. Differential pressure valve returns excess flow to the tank vapor space when discharge pressure exceeds tank pressure by the set differential (typically 1.0 to 1.5 times the pump relief setting minus margin; confirm with the pump datasheet).
  4. Vapor return. Equalising line to the receiving vessel or tanker, with excess-flow check valve.
  5. Discharge. Pneumatic fail-closed ESDV, hydrostatic relief between closed valves, then the filling or distribution header.

Every ESDV is air-to-open, spring-to-close. Loss of air, power or signal drives the valve closed.

UAE regulatory and hazardous area classification

  • Authority. Dubai Civil Defence (DCD) approves design, equipment and final inspection. Follow the UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice and the current AGAC requirements for LPG storage and distribution.
  • Hazardous area. Classify to IEC 60079-10-1. Typical outcome: Zone 1 around pump seals, vents and flanged connections; Zone 2 in the surrounding open-air envelope. Class I Division 1 and 2 apply where NEC/NFPA documents are the project basis.
  • Equipment. ATEX or IECEx certified Ex d / Ex e / Ex i gear matched to gas group IIA (propane, butane) and temperature class T2 or better. Dubai summer ambient up to 52 °C must be inside the certificate ambient range.
  • Codes. NFPA 58 (LP-Gas Code) for storage, transfer and spacing. NFPA 54 (National Fuel Gas Code) for downstream piping and appliances, including high-rise commercial and residential distribution.

PLC / RTU logic and programming

Sequence of operations, ESD cause and effect, and fail-safe pump control code.

Sequence of operations

  1. Pre-start permissives. No ESD or FAP signal, no gas Level 1, suction and discharge ESDV open, tank level above low-level permissive, motor overload healthy.
  2. Auto-venting. Open the vapor vent or bleed valve briefly to clear vapor from the pump casing, close, confirm suction pressure is stable.
  3. Start. Command run, wait for motor feedback within 3 s, then supervise DP and flow.
  4. Lead-lag. Alternate the lead pump on each stop. Start the lag pump if flow demand stays unmet. Fail over if one pump is not ready.
  5. Dry-run protection. Trip on low motor current or low flow with run command for longer than the dry-run timer (typically 5 s).
  6. Minimum flow. DPV bypass handles minimum flow. PLC trips the pump if bypass runs for longer than the allowed time with a closed discharge.
